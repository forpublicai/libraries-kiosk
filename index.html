01 — Why libraries

America already trusts libraries with what comes next.

Public libraries have long served as trusted community centers for information and learning, staffed by information scientists and educators who are stewards of responsible technology access. This is why libraries are already where millions of Americans go when private resources fall short: according to the Public Library Service survey data released in September 2025, public libraries had over 800 million visits in 2025 and over 155 million people accessed job search support, early childhood education, and countless other services at their local libraries.

Since 2011, public libraries have successfully transitioned patrons from passive consumers to active creators through makerspaces — community workshops that gave people access to 3D printers, laser cutters, recording studios, and fabrication tools they could never afford individually. Public AI is the next chapter in that same story: expanding the community toolkit from physical creation to digital and cognitive creation.

03 — The AI kiosk

A shared Public AI workstation, in your library.

Each partner library has access to a cloud-based Public AI workstation, aka the ‘kiosk’, that runs on an existing library computer. Each kiosk offers a curated suite of AI-powered tools. The idea is that instead of each patron paying for AI tools on their own, the library provides shared access in a supportive environment.

AI Chat

for research, learning, and general question answering

Creative Tools

for generating images, music and videos

Code Development

for programming, prototyping, and app development

Educational Resources

including an interactive learning guide and library-vetted educational content

…and this is how we think these tools can help a patron:

  • Small businessA local small business owner can use the chat tool to draft business plans, analyze market trends, or write grant applications.
  • StudentA student can use the same tool to learn about anything, ask any number of clarifying questions, and enhance their understanding of complex concepts.
  • CreatorsStudents and professionals can produce high-quality music or videos, for school, work or personal hobbies, without needing an expensive studio.
  • Local shopA local shop owner can generate high-quality product photos or marketing materials (posters, social media ads) that would otherwise cost thousands in agency fees.
  • BuildersStudents and professionals can get coding support and learn to build websites or simple apps in a short span of time.
$1,000

per kiosk deployment, per year

Participating libraries are asked to contribute $1,000 per kiosk deployment per year. Our kiosk is designed to be easy to set up and use, with minimal technical overhead for library staff.

04 — Privacy & shared-device safety

Built for safe use in a shared, public space.

We prioritize privacy and safe use in shared public environments.

Industry-leading models

We are utilizing industry-leading Large Language Models (LLMs) that adhere to strict data protection and security standards.

A private account each

Every patron gets a dedicated account that ensures their work and history are private to them.

Nothing kept on our platform

Public AI does not store patrons’ conversations or generated content on its own platform.

05 — Beyond the kiosk

What we’re working toward.

Near term

We are focused on measurable outcomes: patrons who walk away having completed a real task such as working on a job application, crafting a business plan, or working on a creative project using AI tools they wouldn’t otherwise have access to. We want library staff to become confident AI guides, reducing the barrier for community members who need a trusted human in the room.

Longer term

We are building toward something more ambitious. As libraries begin to ingest and steward their own community knowledge — local history, local news, local voices — we hope for the kiosk to become a place where local knowledge is preserved and put to work for the people it belongs to. Ultimately, we believe that communities should own and govern their own knowledge and data through public infrastructure.

06 — Our strategic roadmap

From a shared kiosk to community-owned AI.

  1. Phase 1 Now

    The Kiosk

    • Implement a digital interface through which patrons can access different AI services.
  2. Phase 2

    Community Knowledge Infrastructure

    • Deploy infrastructure for Data Sovereignty, allowing libraries to ingest local knowledge/archives (history, local news, events, local personages).
    • Deploy an interface for patrons to engage with the data.
  3. Phase 3

    Democratic Governance

    • Enable libraries to train models on their data.
    • Deploy tools for patrons and librarians to vote on “Community Alignment” for local AI models.
